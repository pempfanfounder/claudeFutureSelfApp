# Manus brief: original quotes for Future Self

Self-contained brief for generating motivational quotes. Everything you need is
in this document — you do not need access to the app or its codebase.

---

## A. What Future Self is

Future Self is a hard-paywalled iOS/Android app that delivers daily sets of
motivational quotes and affirmations. During onboarding, users pick the
categories that resonate with them ("Which voices push you hardest?"), and the
app weights their daily content toward those categories. Content reaches users
in-app, via push notifications, and on home/lock-screen widgets — so every
line must read well out of context, on a lock screen, in front of strangers.
The visual brand is serif type on cream: premium, calm, considered. The words
carry the fire; the design stays quiet.

## B. Your task

Generate **200 original quotes** (quotes only — no affirmations) in the
founder's voice, described below.

- **25 quotes per category**, across these 8 category slugs (exact strings):
  `discipline`, `ambition`, `courage`, `stoic-calm`, `gratitude`,
  `resilience`, `focus`, `kindness`
- Each quote gets **1 or 2 categories**. Count a quote toward the 25-per-category
  quota by its **first** (primary) category; use the optional second category
  freely where a line genuinely spans two.
- The existing library is thin in some categories. Current per-category counts
  in the live library: discipline 29, courage 27, stoic-calm 26, ambition 26,
  resilience 24, focus 20, **gratitude 16, kindness 15**. So when you assign an
  optional second category, prefer the thin ones — `kindness`, `gratitude`,
  `focus`, `resilience` — so the library moves toward parity.
- The reference list (section E) is heavy on discipline/ambition energy. Part
  of your job is translating that same voice into the quieter categories:
  gratitude in this voice sounds like "What a privilege it is to be exhausted
  from work that you used to pray for" (#7); stoic-calm sounds like "We suffer
  more in imagination than in reality" (#2). Same hunger, different target.

## C. Style DNA — the founder's voice

Study section E, then reproduce these patterns. Themes first:

1. **Sacrifice and urgency.** The life you want has a price, and the meter is
   running. — "If you dont sacrifice for the life you want, the life you want
   will become the sacrifice"; "It's now or never"; "You don't have time".
2. **Time scarcity / memento mori.** Death and wasted decades used as fuel, not
   despair. — "The graveyards are full of men who swore they'd start tomorrow"
   (#61); "Everyday your window of opportunity gets smaller and smaller" (#10);
   "Regret exists because time is limited. Regret is a product of scarcity.
   Scarcity of time." (#59).
3. **Regret-avoidance.** The enemy is the older you looking back. — "Don't be
   the 35 year old man wondering what he even did with his 20s" (#19); "…laying
   on your death bed realizing you still had more gas left in the tank" (#62).
4. **Proving doubters wrong.** Success as the answer to everyone who dismissed
   you. — "Do it because they said you couldn't" (#55); "Never let less
   successful people tell you what your limit is" (#14); "Success is the
   ultimate revenge." (#45).
5. **Inner-kid motivation.** Doing it for the younger self who needed someone.
   — "Do it for the scared kid" (#23); "Do it for the kid who felt alone,
   unheard, and forgotten" (#24); "Do it for the kid who needed someone to
   believe in him" (#26).
6. **Discipline over comfort.** Comfort is the opponent. — "Comfort will deter
   you from your goal. Don't be stuck in comfort." (#56); "Don't wish for it,
   work for it." (#51); "Pick yourself up, and get back to work" (#41).
7. **Winning as identity.** Refusing to let the story end on a loss. — "It's
   not over until I win" (#27); "Simply refuse to end on a loss. One day you
   will win." (#58); "A winner is just a loser who tried one more time." (#4).

Voice:

- **Second-person direct address** — the line talks straight at the reader:
  "You have to want it more than your fear wants you to stop" (#60); "Remember
  who you are" (#42).
- **First-person resolve** — occasionally the reader speaks the vow: "It's not
  over until I win" (#27); "If I play, I play to win" (#54); "I just want to
  make my people proud" (#49).
- **Short declaratives** — compressed, no hedging: "Success is a decision"
  (#8); "The world is yours" (#21); "Chase something bigger." (#57).
- **Occasional casual interjection** — a rare "bro"-style aside that makes it
  feel like a friend, not a poster: "Bro, I am counting on you." (#52); "In
  another life? No bro, this one. Get after it." (#16). Use this flavor
  sparingly (a handful of lines at most) and keep it gender-neutral.
- **No attribution** — every line is original; nothing is quoted from anyone.

Sentence shapes (mix all of these across the 200):

1. **Aphorism** — a compressed claim that sounds like law: "Until death, all
   defeat is psychological" (#1); "Success is a decision" (#8).
2. **If/then conditional** — cause and consequence: "If you dont master your
   time, someone else will." (#17); "Chase happiness and you'll end up on the
   dopamine treadmill. Chase being proud of yourself and you'll end up happy."
   (#44).
3. **Imperative** — a direct command: "Pick yourself up, and get back to work"
   (#41); "Give something your all" (#20).
4. **Rhetorical question / binary fork** — two roads, pick one: "Are you gonna
   let the opportunity pass by, or are you gonna give it all you can?" (#6);
   "We can stay here, or we can go up" (#15); "Some wake up at 25, some at 16.
   Most? They never wake up." (#46).
5. **Vivid future-scene** — a concrete flash of the life ahead or the day of
   reckoning: "There will come a day when your body can't keep up with your
   ambition…" (#31); "Dad's getting older, mom's getting tired. It's now or
   never." (#36).
6. **Contrast flip** — you vs. the person who simply acted: "While you are
   overthinking, someone … is becoming successful just by trying." (#64);
   "There are men who had nothing last year - they took action, and now they
   are running empires." (#30) — keep your versions gender-neutral (see D).

Hard rules (every line must pass all of these):

- **4–30 words** per quote.
- **Second person or first-person resolve.** No third-person lecture tone.
- **English.** No hashtags, no emoji, no ALL CAPS.
- **No stock clichés** — nothing like "believe and achieve", "reach for the
  stars", "sky's the limit", "hustle harder", "dream big".
- **Original lines only.** Never quote or paraphrase living authors, song
  lyrics, films, or any copyrighted material. Note that a few lines in the
  reference list echo famous quotes (#2 is Seneca; #61 is a well-known trope) —
  do not resubmit those or close variants; write genuinely new lines.
- **No fabricated attribution.** The app's rule: quotes are either original
  (author is null) or public-domain with accurate attribution. Yours are all
  original, so `author` stays `null` on every item. Never invent an author.
- **No duplicates or near-duplicates** — of the reference list, of the famous
  quotes it echoes, or of each other.

## D. Brand guardrails

Future Self ships in the App Store at a 4+ age rating with a calm, premium
brand. Capture the hunger, the urgency, the memento-mori edge of the reference
list — that energy is the product — but adapt these specific patterns. These
are adaptations for a public app with a broad audience, not censorship of the
founder's private list:

- **No money-flex specifics.** Nothing like "100k months" or "you just made
  8.7 million". Ambition and building something great: yes. Dollar amounts and
  income flexing: no.
- **No put-downs of women, exes, or romantic scorekeeping.** Skip the pattern
  of #13, #29, and #48 entirely — no "she better cook…", no "most successful
  guy she's ever talked to", no framing success as leverage over a partner.
- **No "dumber than you" insults.** The contrast-flip shape is great, but the
  other person is never inferior — they just *tried*. "While you're
  overthinking, someone is winning just by trying" works; "someone dumber than
  you" does not.
- **No religious claims presented as fact.** "God gave you that dream for a
  reason" only works reframed as personal conviction or secular framing —
  e.g. "You didn't get that dream by accident" — never as a factual claim
  about God.
- **Nothing that reads as medical or mental-health advice.** No lines about
  curing anxiety or depression, no "you don't need therapy, you need X", no
  health prescriptions.
- **Gender-neutral by default.** The app has users of all genders. "The kid
  who doubted himself" becomes "the kid who doubted themselves" or "the kid
  who…" with no pronoun; "men who had nothing" becomes "people who had
  nothing"; "a man that doesnt keep his word" becomes "a person who doesn't
  keep their word", and so on.
- **Lock-screen safe.** Every line may appear in a push notification or on a
  lock screen. Nothing that would alarm, shame, or embarrass a user if a
  colleague glanced at their phone.

## F. Output format

Return **one single JSON array** of 200 objects, nothing else. Each item:

```json
{
  "type": "quote",
  "body": "The quote text.",
  "author": null,
  "categories": ["discipline", "focus"]
}
```

- `type` is always `"quote"`.
- `author` is always `null` (all lines are original — see section C).
- `categories` is an array of 1 or 2 slugs. Valid slugs, exactly:
  `discipline`, `ambition`, `courage`, `stoic-calm`, `gratitude`,
  `resilience`, `focus`, `kindness`. The first slug is the primary category
  (25 quotes per primary category).

After the JSON array, add **one line** of self-check confirming: every body is
4–30 words; there are no duplicates or near-duplicates of the reference list
or of each other; every category is one of the 8 valid slugs; every author is
null; each of the 8 categories has exactly 25 primary quotes.
